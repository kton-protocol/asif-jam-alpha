Linear extrapolation of the balanced-panel annual fog rate, 1990-2024.

nebel: -0.1704 pp/yr, intercept 9.09 -> crosses zero in 2043
gew:   -0.1321 pp/yr, intercept 9.93 -> crosses zero in 2065

The second line is the price of the first. Nothing in the data distinguishes the two
extrapolations; they are the same arithmetic on two columns of the same rows. If
'fog is abolished by 2043' is a finding, then 'thunderstorms are abolished by 2065' is
the same finding, and it is absurd. A linear fit to a bounded, strictly positive quantity
has a zero crossing by construction, whatever the data are.

A constant-proportional-decline model on the same points (log-linear, -3.00%/yr)
fits the observed rates slightly WORSE (R2 on the original scale 0.688 vs 0.728)
and never reaches zero at all:
   2030: linear  +2.27%   log-linear  2.89%
   2043: linear  +0.06%   log-linear  1.94%
   2050: linear  -1.14%   log-linear  1.57%
   2070: linear  -4.54%   log-linear  0.85%

The two models are 0.728 vs 0.688 on the same scale -- a difference of 4.0% of the
variance, which is not a basis on which to prefer either. Both are fitted to the same 35
numbers. Choosing between them is a choice about what fog is, not a result read off the
file, and the two disagree about whether the claim in the TASK is true at all.
